# WI-10 · The guided workflow: from "something is wrong here" to a tested map

**Purpose:** take one trouble spot through eight steps, from finding out whether it is the place or one robot, to a fixed map that has been tried on one AMR and rolled out.

The **Guided** tab is the first tab on the right. The step bar across the top shows where you are; a finished step can be clicked to go back. Every investigation is saved as a **case** in the left panel, so a colleague can open it and carry on.

**You need:** the fleet manager set up once for this workspace ([[WI-09]] part A). From step 4 on: at least one recording that drives through the box ([[WI-01]] C).

> The program only reads from robots and servers. Steps 7 and 8 say "you load the map": that is done in RoboShop, never by this program.

## The steps

| Step | You do | The program does |
|---|---|---|
| ① Where and when | Choose the map, draw a box around the trouble area, pick a time (6 hours to 7 days back, or custom). Optionally name the robot the question is about. | Reads the fleet manager's history for **every** robot that drove through the box. |
| ② What happened | Read. | Shows passes (fine, low, bad), each robot's lowest confidence, stops, relocalisations and errors, and patterns: direction of travel, time of day, before and after a map change. The trips are drawn on the map. |
| ③ Cause | Read the verdict. | Gives one verdict with a rating and the reasons. If it is not a map problem, the case can be closed here. |
| ④ Evidence | Tick the recordings that drive through the box, and the other map versions to compare with. | Lays the lasers over the map: how well each version fits, which walls were seen, and what is seen that is not in the map. |
| ⑤ Fix options | Choose an option and name the new file. | Offers only the fixes that fit the evidence and says why the others are not offered. |
| ⑥ Build and prove | Press **Build**. | Writes the new map as a file in `candidates`, runs every check, and compares the same lasers through the old and the new map. |
| ⑦ Trial on one AMR | Load the file on one AMR in RoboShop, record a drive through the box, press **Judge the trial**. | Confirms the robot runs that exact file, then gives PASS or FAIL against the numbers from before. |
| ⑧ Roll out and watch | Load the file on all AMRs. A day or two later press **Compare with before**. | Confirms every robot has the same file, and compares the same box before and after. |

## The verdicts in step ③

| Verdict | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| The place: weak for the whole fleet | On a typical pass robots fall below 0.5 here. | Step ④. |
| The place: marginal | Robots usually get through, but many have had a bad pass. | Step ④. |
| The place: worse since (date) | Confidence dropped around a map change. The change can be the cause, or something was moved on the floor at the same time. | Step ④, with the version from before the change ticked. |
| One robot, at this place | Only one or two robots had trouble; others with several passes were fine. | Look at what happened at that moment ([[WI-09]]), not at the map. |
| One robot, everywhere | That robot is also low in the rest of the plant. | Check its scanners: clean, level, at the right height ([[KB-09]]). |
| One-off events | A few troubled passes with no pattern. | Things passing through. No map fix. |
| Not enough data | Fewer than 5 passes or fewer than 2 robots. | Longer time or larger box. |

The **rating** (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW) comes from the number of robots and passes, never from a guess: HIGH needs 5 robots and 30 passes, MEDIUM 3 robots and 10 passes.

## Step ④ in detail

Steps 1 to 3 show **that** robots have trouble in the box. Step ④ finds **which walls** are wrong. For that it needs laser scans taken inside the box, and those are not collected all the time: a recording has to be made.

**Recorded automatically by the apps server.** If the workspace names an AMR Health server (History sources) that collects laser scans, this card lists the scans taken near the box, per robot. It is filled by itself once you have signed in; the first time press **Sign in …** and give your AMR Health user name and password (kept encrypted for your Windows user). **Compare the stored scans with the map** places each scan on the map and shows:

- each robot's share of laser points on walls. A robot clearly below the others sees the same place worse: check its scanners (clean, level, height);
- the walls of the map that were seen, and what is seen that is not in the map.

Scans exist only for the robots named in the AMR Health settings, and only where such a robot was at a sample time or had a problem. A single scan cannot show a position jump: for that a drive is still needed (part A).

**A · Recordings that drive through this box.** Only recordings that really pass through the box are listed, with their passes, direction and lowest confidence. If there is none:

2. Leave **all robots** ticked (or tick only some) and press **Record them when they drive through**. Each robot is recorded the first time it comes near the box, and its recording stops by itself. The table shows what each robot is doing. Keep the window open; **Stop watching** ends it and keeps what was recorded.
3. Send the robot the usual way, with the fleet manager. This program never sends a robot anywhere.

**B · Compare what the robots see with the map.** Map versions that have the same walls in the box are grouped, so only versions that really differ there are offered. If an earlier version matches the lasers better, its walls are a fix. The result also says whether the recording shows a **position jump**: that means a whole piece of the map is misplaced.

## Reading the evidence in step ④

| Colour on the map | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green | Wall in the map that the lasers saw. |
| Red | Wall in the map that the lasers did not see: gone, or hidden behind something. |
| Blue | Seen by the lasers in every recording, not in the map: something permanent was added. |
| Pink | Seen in some recordings only: something passing through or put down for a while. |

- A good map gives 50 to 75 % in "how well each version fits". A version that fits at least 5 points better than the map in question becomes a fix option.
- With one recording a parked bin and a new wall look the same. A second recording made at another time tells them apart.
- A place where things come and go cannot be made right in a map.

## Moving a whole misplaced piece (step ⑤ and ⑥)

When the recording shows a position jump, **Fix options** offers **Move a whole misplaced piece**. This is the method that fixed the reference case ([[KB-03]]).

1. **Recording that drives the whole piece.** Best is one drive from the part that is right, through the misplaced piece, and back.
2. **Find the rigid pieces.** Each row is a stretch over which the map is rigid against the lasers; "step" and "turn" say how it sits against the row above. Tick the piece to **move** and the part that is **right**. The program cannot know which side is right: the right part is the one that looks the same in older versions of the map.
3. **Measure the turn.** The rotation is given with its spread.
4. **Draw the region** around everything that belongs to the misplaced piece (the orange track and the walls, stations and paths beside it). Optionally choose an older map whose walls must stay.
5. **Build and prove.** All checks must pass and the replay must show no jump.
6. **Repair what is left at the seam**, if jumps remain: where the piece meets the rest, old walls of both placements overlap. The program proposes the stretch, finds the version whose walls fit the lasers best there, and takes them.

Going on to the trial needs all checks passed and no jump left.

## When Build refuses to go on

If the new walls would put a path or station too close to a wall, the checks fail and step ⑥ stops. That is the sign of a **misplaced piece of map**: the walls belong somewhere else than the stations that go with them. A box cannot fix that; it only moves the jump ([[KB-03]]). Go back to **Fix options** and choose **Move a whole misplaced piece**.

## Limits

- Steps ① to ③ use the fleet history, which has no laser data.
- Step ④ needs a recording through the box. Laser data is not collected all the time.
- Guided fixes are: walls in a box taken from another map version, and moving a whole misplaced piece. Hand edits are on the **Edit** tab.
- An armed recording only runs while this program is open.
- The scanner height the robot reports is a setting. If a scanner was moved without changing the setting, only the comparison between robots shows it.
