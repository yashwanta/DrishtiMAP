# KB-03 · The Springfield case

This is the case the method in this program comes from. The numbers are the program's own results on the Springfield files.

## The problem

Robots jumped near LM519–LM521. Replaying a recorded loop through the live map shows it: the map placement steps **1.36 m at LM520**. The same drive does not fit the live map as one rigid track (0.62 m rms; a good map gives about 0.1 m).

## Three attempts that did not fix it

| Map | What was done | Result |
|---|---|---|
| v3 | Part of the east loop corrected | Jump at LM519–LM521 gone; a new step of about 2 m at **LM441–LM443** |
| v3 with a rescan | The LM441–LM443 area scanned again on top of v3 | Step still there |
| v4 | Another partial correction around LM441–LM443 | Step still there |

Each attempt worked on the place where the jump showed. Each time the jump moved to the new edge of what had been changed.

## The cause

**Find rigid pieces** on the live map shows the east loop is one rigid piece, 73 m long, agreeing with the lasers to 0.09 m, and turned about 2 degrees against the west part of the map. Nothing inside the east loop is wrong. The whole loop is attached at the wrong angle.

At 40 m from the pivot, 2 degrees is 1.4 m. That is the size of the jump.

The earlier fixes moved part of the loop. The part that was moved now disagreed with the part that was not, so the step appeared between them, at LM441–LM443.

## The fix

1. **Measure turn** with samples inside the east loop and samples on the correct part: 2.07 degrees, spread ±0.17 (v5 was built with 2.19).
2. One region drawn around the whole east loop, cutting the route near the pivot.
3. **Build → Move a region**: walls, stations, paths, feature lines, areas and bins of the loop moved as one block.

Result (v5): replay shows **no jump**, the drive fits as one rigid track at 0.09 m, every check passes except one feature line (below). The trial on one robot ran through LM519–LM521 and LM441–LM443 without a jump.

## What still went wrong, and what it taught

| What happened | Lesson | Now in the program |
|---|---|---|
| A feature line ended up 0.12 m from path LM514–LM515. The robot stopped there with a virtual-laser fault for 20 s. | A geometry fix can leave a line in a robot's way. | Check "no feature line closer than 0.45 m to a path"; **Detect** marks it. [[KB-06]] |
| The line was removed and a path direction changed on the robot. Later the robot was found running the earlier file again, under the fleet's map name. | A map name says nothing about the content. | **Check maps on all robots** compares checksums. [[WI-07]] |
| A first test method judged each scan on its own. Shifting its internal grid by 1.3 cm flipped the same map between "clean" and "lost". | A test must give the same answer when nothing real changes. | Replay matches 8 m stretches and is checked at several grid placements. [[KB-04]] |
| Low confidence between LM463 and LM513 | Bins stand there; they are not map walls. | Low wall match is a warning, not a fault. |

## The short version

- The jump is at the seam. The cause is the whole piece behind it.
- Find the piece first. Move all of it, once.
- Prove it with a replay before the robot, and with one robot before the fleet.
- Build from the file that is on the robot today.
