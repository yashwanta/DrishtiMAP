# KB-09 · The LiDAR scanners, their height, and how to look at a scan

## What the scanners are

Each robot carries one or more 2D laser scanners (LiDAR). A 2D scanner sweeps a beam in **one flat plane** and measures the distance to the first thing each beam hits. It sees whatever crosses that plane and nothing above or below it.

The robot reports, for each scanner, where it is mounted and the **height of its scan plane above the floor**. On the reference plant's robots:

| Scanner | Type | Position | Height of the scan plane | Field of view | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `laser` | FocueRay h100 | front, 0.43 m ahead of the robot's centre | 0.194 m | 180° | 40 m |
| `laser2` | FocueRay c200 | rear, 0.41 to 0.44 m behind the centre | 0.197 m | 180° | 50 m |

Other robots and other plants can differ. The program always shows what the robot itself reports.

## Why the height matters

- The map's wall dots are what the scanners saw **at that height** when the map was made. A rack shows as its legs, not its shelves. A table shows as its legs. A pallet on the floor shows as a block; the same pallet lifted above 0.2 m is invisible.
- Anything that changes at scanner height changes what the robot sees: pallets and bins set down, low carts, floor-level trim. Things that change only above it do not.
- If two robots had scanners at different heights, they would see different outlines in the same place. Compare the heights when one robot behaves differently from the others.

## Look at a scan

On the **Tools** tab, section **LiDAR · what each scanner sees**:

1. Choose a **Recording** and a **Map**.
2. Press **Show sample**. The scan is drawn on the map, each scanner in its own colour, with the robot's outline and heading.
3. Step with the ◀ ▶ buttons, the slider, or the arrow keys (Shift = 10 samples).
4. The panel lists each scanner with the height of its scan plane, its field of view, and how many beams hit something.

**Live from robot** does the same with the robot's current scan, refreshed about once a second. It only reads.

## How to read it

| You see | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scan points on the grey walls | The map agrees with what the scanner sees. |
| Scan points where the map has nothing | Something at scanner height that is not in the map: a pallet, a bin, a person's legs. |
| Grey walls in view with no scan points on them | The map has something the scanner does not see: removed, or the map is wrong there. |
| The whole scan shifted or turned against the walls | The robot's reported position is off at that moment, or you are showing a different map than the robot was using. |

> The scan is placed at the position the robot **reported**. On the map the robot was using, that shows how well it was localised. On another map (for example a candidate) the points can sit off the walls simply because the map differs; use **Replay** to test a candidate.
