# KB-04 · Reading the numbers

## Replay

The program first rebuilds the true shape of the drive from the lasers alone, scan against scan, without any map. This is the **laser track**. Then it takes the scans of about 8 m of driving as one rigid cloud and finds where that cloud fits the map best. It does this every 1.5 m along the drive.

| Number | Meaning | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **step** | How far the best fit of one stretch is from the fit of the stretch before, at the robot | under 0.25 m | 0.5 m or more = a jump |
| **wall match** | Share of laser points that land on a map wall | 55 % and up | under 40 % = robots report low confidence |
| **turn** (at a step) | How much the fit rotates between the two stretches | near 0 | a degree or more with a step = a piece at an angle |

Steps marked **ambiguous** are places where a different position fits almost as well (a long plain aisle looks the same a metre along). Steps marked **track restart** are where the laser track itself breaks, because the robot was relocalised or samples are missing. Neither is counted as a map fault.

## Fits as one rigid track

The whole laser track is laid on the map with a single turn and shift, and the remaining error is reported.

| rms | Meaning |
|---|---|
| about 0.1 m | The map agrees with the building along this drive. |
| 0.15 to 0.3 m | Something is slightly off; look at the pieces. |
| above 0.3 m | Part of the map is in the wrong place. |

This catches a misplaced piece even when no single step reaches 0.5 m, for example when the error builds up slowly.

> This test is only fair on a drive without long stops, up to roughly 120 m. On a longer drive, or one with many stops and turns on the spot, the laser track itself drifts by a few degrees and the single fit gets worse although the map is fine. The trial drive of the Springfield fix (140 m, driven by hand) gives 0.8 m rms on a map whose replay shows no step at all. Then judge by the replay steps and by **Find rigid pieces**: long pieces at about 0.1 m rms mean the map is right inside each piece.

## Find rigid pieces

Each piece is a stretch of the drive that fits the map rigidly. For each seam between pieces:

- **step at seam**: how far the two pieces disagree where they meet.
- **turn**: the rotation of this piece against the one before.

Two long pieces with a turn between them **and** a jump in the replay at their seam is the signature of a piece attached at an angle.

A turn of a few degrees at a seam without a replay jump, at a place where the robot stopped or turned on the spot, is drift of the laser track. A seam after a gap of unmatched scans (for example an area full of bins) is also not evidence: the step there is a guess across the gap.

## Measure turn

| Number | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **turn** | The rotation that puts the piece back |
| **spread** | Half the range of three repeated measurements. Treat the turn as that uncertain. |
| **about (x, y)** | The pivot: the point that does not move |
| **th, tx, ty** | The same correction as the build tool needs it: `x' = cos·x − sin·y + tx` |

What the spread means on the floor: ±0.17 degrees is ±3 mm per metre from the pivot, so ±0.12 m at 40 m. If the spread is above 0.3 degrees, use longer sample ranges or a better recording.

## Recording summary

Taken from what the robot itself reported, no lasers: stations passed, confidence at each, jumps of the reported position, and abnormal localisation states with their time and place.

## Why results are repeated

The map's wall dots sit on a 5 cm grid, and any matching method lays its own grid over them. A method is only trusted here if it gives the same verdict when that grid is shifted by a centimetre or two. The replay is tested that way; the turn is measured at three grid placements and reported with the spread.
